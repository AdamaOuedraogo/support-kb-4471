1 · parser — the shared parser reads month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first (checked against the platform’s locale data, not a typed list). The shared parser reads MM/DD, so a day of 1 to 12 swaps with the month: a booking for 7 April goes out as 4 July. Days above 12 cannot be read month first, which is why no report quotes one.

This is every report that came in through the app or by email, plus form reports from booking-v2 tenants, whose form prints the right DD/MM/YYYY hint. It is what #4471 was meant to be, and the queued fix (parse with the tenant’s locale) covers it.

2 · hint — booking-v3 tells day-first tenants to type month first

The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Three day-first tenants run it (alder.physio, copper.studio, pale.harbour). Their customers type what the hint says, month first, and the form reads it the tenant’s way, day first. The symptom is the same month flip, so the subject lines match and the desk merged them, but the cause is in the form, not the parser. Fixing the parser leaves this in place; the hint must follow the tenant’s locale, or the field must be read in the order the hint shows.

3 · one-day-move — the date shifts by one day

A few reports describe no month flip at all. The date moves by exactly one day, the same meeting lands on different days in Lisbon and Toronto, or an invite is stamped before the booking was made. That is a date-boundary problem: the booking’s date crosses a time-zone offset or the UTC day line between booking and invite. The desk had read these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) and filed them under parser by tenant, footnoted, because their subjects matched. They keep that place in the count and get a ticket of their own, because the parser fix will not move them back.

Why one ticket swallowed three problems

  • Dedupe by subject (decision 0001) merges on the subject line alone. Twelve subject lines cover all three problems, so matching subjects said “same issue” every time.
  • Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser locale. A tenant in Germany whose staff run an en-US browser was tagged hint; a booking-v3 form report from a Portuguese browser was tagged parser. The table above shows how often the tag and the cause disagree. The cause depends on the tenant and the channel, which intake never looks up.
  • The page was written from the queue, not from the data. The incident page typed “one issue” into a paragraph while its own table, built from the data, already showed two causes and a footnote.

What the desk should do

  1. Keep #4471 as the parser ticket, with the count in the first row above.
  2. Track the hint and one-day-move problems on their own tickets (linked in the table).
  3. Change triage so a merge checks the tenant’s locale and form, not the subject alone. The dedupe explorer already runs that check; make it the rule.
